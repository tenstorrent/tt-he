# exp_Tg000 — a correct CKKS ct_mul at the exp_Tb001 ring, verified by decrypt + decode

`host/experiments/exp_tg000.cpp` · shares `kernels/experiments/exp_tb001/` · `./build/exp_tg000 [seed]`

## Why this exists

The repo had two experiments at this ring and neither was a CKKS scheme.

| | what it is | what it proves |
|---|---|---|
| [`exp_tb001.md`](exp_tb001.md) | a **cost model** of the CROSS Set-B multiply | device == CPU oracle, 7.494 ms critical path busy |
| [`exp_tb001b.md`](exp_tb001b.md) | a semantically correct **BFV** multiply | `decrypt(ct_mul) == m₁·m₂` in `R_t` |
| **exp_Tg000** | a semantically correct **CKKS** multiply | `decode(decrypt(ct_mul)) ≈ z₁ ⊙ z₂` |

The gap is specific. exp_Tb001's op graph is already *CKKS-shaped* — pointwise tensor, drop-prime
rescale, `dnum` digit key-switch — but its inputs are random residues and its evaluation key is
random, so "correct" there means "bit-identical to a CPU oracle running the same arithmetic".
Nothing in it encrypts or decrypts. exp_Tb001b does encrypt and decrypt, but BFV is a different
scheme: its tensor happens over ℤ in an extended base and its rescale is `⌊t·e/Q⌉`.

So exp_Tb001 has CKKS's *shape* without its *semantics*, and exp_Tb001b has semantics for the wrong
scheme. exp_Tg000 is the missing corner, and it needed **no new kernel** to build.

## Parameters

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| `N` | 8192 | the CROSS Set-B ring, same as the other two |
| slots | 4096 | CKKS packs `N/2` **complex** slots into a real polynomial |
| `Q` | 8 × 28-bit = **224-bit** | ciphertext modulus |
| `P` | 4 × 28-bit | key-switch base |
| `dnum` | 2 | two 4-limb digits, as exp_Tb001 |
| `Δ` | **`q₆·q₇` = 71688593531928577 ≈ 2⁵⁶** | the scaling factor |
| I/O | **evaluation form** | as exp_Tb001; `TG000_COEFF_IO=1` for the other contract |

`Δ` is not a round power of two — it **is** the product of the primes the rescale drops. Then the
product's scale `Δ²` divided by those same primes lands back on `Δ` exactly, so the output scale
equals the input scale with no drift and nothing to renormalise.

Using **two** primes rather than one is the headline precision decision, and it is what lets a
28-bit kernel carry a 56-bit scale without touching the kernel — see *Precision* below.
`TG000_NDROP=1` gives the one-prime configuration back for an A/B in the same binary.

There is no `S` base and no plaintext modulus `t`. Both are BFV-only.

## What makes CKKS cheaper than BFV on the same kernels

The whole of exp_Tb001b's base-extension apparatus disappears:

| step | BFV (exp_Tb001b) | CKKS (here) |
|---|---|---|
| tensor | lift `Q → Q\|S\|m_sk` (18 limbs), exact Shenoy–Kumaresan, centred sentinel | **pointwise mod Q**, 8 limbs, 4 modmuls |
| rescale | `⌊t·e/Q⌉` — divide by the *whole* modulus, needs the un-reduced tensor | **drop a prime** — `bconv_one` + modsub + modmul |
| base conversion | exact at the boundary, approximate in the interior | approximate everywhere, and that is provably fine |

CKKS never lifts the ciphertext out of its own base: the product is taken mod `Q` and the coefficient
growth is absorbed by the rescale afterwards. That removes the auxiliary base, the exact conversion,
the sentinel limb, and `baseconv_sk.cpp` entirely. The only general base conversion left is the
key-switch's, and it is the same approximate FBC exp_Tb001 uses.

## The scheme

```
encode(z)    m = round(Δ · σ⁻¹(z))                σ = the canonical embedding
encrypt(m)   c0 = −a·s + e + m ,  c1 = a          Δ lives inside m; there is no Δ/t factor
decrypt(ct)  z = σ( [c0 + c1·s]_Q / scale )
multiply     tensor mod Q → relinearise over Q|P → combine → rescale (drop q₇, q₆)
```

**Rescale last**, which is the canonical CKKS ordering and differs from exp_Tb001's cost model. That
one rescales its four *inputs* first, modelling a chain where operands arrive at scale `Δ²`; the
key-switch there therefore runs on 11 limbs (7 Q + 4 P). Rescaling last is what makes the output
scale equal the input scale — the thing "end to end" needs — and it uses the **full 12-limb QP**,
which is the nominal CROSS Set-B configuration.

### The encoder is written from the definition, not as a fast transform

`R = ℤ[x]/(x^N+1)` embeds into `ℂ^{N/2}` by evaluating at half the primitive 2N-th roots of unity.
`(ℤ/2N)*` is generated by 5 and −1, so `{ζ^(5^j)}` for `j < N/2` picks exactly one root from each
conjugate pair, and a real polynomial is determined by its values there. That map is a **ring
homomorphism**, which is the entire reason CKKS multiplies slot-wise: a polynomial product *is* the
slot-wise product of the embeddings.

The inverse follows from `V*V = N·I` for `V[g][k] = ζ^(gk)` with `g` over the `N` units of `ℤ/2N`:

```
a_k = (2/N) · Σ_j Re( z_j · ζ^(−5^j·k) )
```

Both directions are the **O(N²/2) direct sums**, not the O(N log N) "special FFT". At N=8192 that is
33.5M terms and a fraction of a second, paid a handful of times per run. It is the definition, so it
cannot be subtly wrong the way a hand-rolled bit-reversed radix-2 with a `5^j` ordering can — and
this experiment is the correctness reference. `[0]` checks the round trip at 1.3e-14.

### The relinearisation key uses CRT idempotents

Textbooks write digit `j`'s key as `Q̂_j · [Q̂_j⁻¹]_{D_j}`, which needs a **112-bit modular inverse**.
That integer is exactly the CRT idempotent `w_j`: ≡ 1 mod every prime of `group[j]`, ≡ 0 mod every
other prime of `Q`. Characterised that way its `Q`-residues are 0/1 by definition, and one exact CRT
reconstruction gives the integer whose residues mod the `P` primes the key also needs. No wide
modular inverse is ever computed.

`evk_j = ( −a_j·s + e_j + P·w_j·s² , a_j )` over `QP`.

### Why `dnum=2` is not optional here

With `dnum=1` the key-switch noise is roughly `Q/P` times the key's error. BFV survives that: its
`Δ_BFV = Q/t ≈ 2²¹⁰`, so a 2¹¹² noise term is still far below `Q/2t` — which is why exp_Tb001b gets
away with a single digit. CKKS at `Δ = 2²⁸` does not. Splitting `Q` into two 4-limb digits makes each
digit ≈ 2¹¹² and `P` ≈ 2¹¹², so the ratio is ≈ 1 and the noise stays small. Measured contribution:
~10² in the phase at scale `Δ²`, i.e. nothing after the rescale.

### Why approximate base conversion is safe in both places

The mod-up produces `ĉ_j = [c]_{D_j} + ε_j·D_j` rather than `[c]_{D_j}`. That `ε_j` is never
corrected, and it does not need to be:

* against the key's `P·w_j·s²` term — `D_j·w_j ≡ 0 mod Q` by the idempotent property, so the whole
  `ε_j·D_j·P·w_j·s²` term vanishes mod `Q` after the division by `P`;
* against the key's error term — `ε_j·D_j·e_j / P` with `D_j/P ≈ 1` and `ε_j ≤ 4` is ~10³, negligible
  at scale `Δ²`.

The mod-down's `ε_P ≤ 4` lands directly in the phase at scale `Δ²`, i.e. ~10⁻⁸ after the rescale.

So the host **models the approximation** rather than computing the exact conversion. That is what
lets device and host be compared **bit-exactly**: a fault localises to a stage instead of surfacing
as a precision loss three stages later.

## Precision: where the bits actually go

CKKS is approximate, so `[3]` and `[D7]` are tolerances, not bit-comparisons — which makes the
tolerance the interesting number, not a formality.

| test | max slot error | rms | effective bits (rms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[1]` encrypt → decrypt | 1.1e-14 | 3.5e-15 | 48.0 |
| `[2]` ct_add | 1.8e-14 | 5.0e-15 | 47.5 |
| `[3]`/`[D7]` **ct_mul** | 1.68e-13 | 2.70e-14 | **45.2** |

`TG000_NOISE=1` reports the distribution. It is worth reading rather than trusting, because getting
here took two fixes that the textbook noise analysis does not point at.

### The measurement that found them

The one-prime, non-centred configuration measured **16.4 bits rms** — and a **max/rms of 40**, where
Gaussian noise over 4096 slots gives ≈ 4. The tail was not spread out; it was four slots:

```
#0 worst slot j=0      5^j mod 2N = 1       err=4.677e-04
#1 worst slot j=3786   5^j mod 2N = 16377   err=2.058e-04
#2 worst slot j=1      5^j mod 2N = 5       err=1.712e-04
#3 worst slot j=1700   5^j mod 2N = 16369   err=9.634e-05
```

Every one has `5^j mod 2N` near 0 or near 2N. That is the signature of a **coherent** low-frequency
component in the coefficient-domain error, because `Σ_k ζ^(gk) = −2/(ζ^g − 1)` has magnitude ≈
`2N/(πg)` — so a slowly-varying error is amplified ~5000× in exactly those slots and ~1× everywhere
else.

Inverting the slot error back to coefficients (the slot error *is* the embedding of the coefficient
error, so this is exact and needs no convolution) named the source:

| source | size |
|---|---|
| LWE cross terms `(m₁e₂ + m₂e₁)/Δ` — *the textbook term* | 1.64 units of `1/Δ` |
| **rescale residual × `s`** | **21.39 units** |
| measured, one-prime non-centred | 34.64 units, mean 0.82, max/rms 40 |

The rescale computes `(c − [c]_{q_L})·q_L⁻¹` with `[c]_{q_L}` taken in `[0, q_L)`. That residual is
≤ 1 unit per coefficient — negligible until you notice it lands on **`c₁` as well as `c₀`**, and
`c₁`'s residual is multiplied by `s` at decryption. Convolution with a ternary `s` amplifies it to
`√(‖s‖²/12) ≈ 21` units, 13× the term the analysis predicts, and the non-centred residual's nonzero
*mean* is the coherent part the small-`5^j` slots amplify into the tail.

`TG000_ZEROERR=1` (drop the LWE error entirely) left `[3]` unchanged, confirming the encryption noise
was never what dominated.

### Fix 1 — centring, which needs no kernel

Centring the remainder removes the mean and halves the variance. The obvious implementation is a new
`bconv_one` variant that returns a signed remainder. It is not needed, because of an identity:

```
[c + qHalf]_{q_L}  =  [c]_{q_L} + qHalf − q_L·[ [c]_{q_L} > qHalf ]
```

so running the **plain, unmodified** `[0, q_L)` rescale on `c + qHalf` subtracts exactly the *centred*
remainder — the shift cancels. Adding `qHalf` to every limb before the rescale is therefore identical
to centring it, and costs one `modadd_map` per surviving limb. `bconv_one.cpp` is untouched and the
kernel tree gains no divergence. (Verified exhaustively on random cases including the real 28-bit
primes; the check is in the commit.)

In the evaluation domain the shift is the same operation with a different constant: adding `qHalf` to
every *coefficient* means adding the polynomial `qHalf·(1 + x + … + x^{N−1})`, whose transform is not
constant, so it is computed once on the host and uploaded rather than filled. Same dispatch count.

### Fix 2 — a two-prime scale, which needs no kernel either

Centring alone leaves the floor at `√(‖s‖²/12) ≈ 21` units **at the output scale**. That is a fixed
number of units, so the way to make it small is to make the unit small: widen `Δ`.

`Δ` cannot exceed 28 bits *as a prime* — the kernels' int8 radix-128 decomposition fixes that. But
nothing requires `Δ` to be one prime. Setting `Δ = q₆·q₇ ≈ 2⁵⁶` and dropping **both** in the rescale
moves the same 21 units 28 bits further down, and the first step's residual is then divided by the
second prime, so it does not accumulate. Pure host change.

### The result, and the confirmation

| | one-prime, non-centred | two-prime, centred |
|---|---|---|
| coefficient error sd | 34.64 units | **21.42 units** (predicted 21.39) |
| coefficient error mean | 0.82 units | **−0.05 units** |
| max/rms | 39.9 | **6.2** |
| max slot error | 4.68e-4 (11.1 bits) | **1.68e-13 (42.4 bits)** |
| rms slot error | 1.17e-5 (16.4 bits) | **2.70e-14 (45.2 bits)** |

**+31.3 bits of max-error precision**, and the residual lands on the predicted floor to three
significant figures. The 3.7 bits from centring and the 28 from the wider scale are additive, as the
model says they should be.

One honest limit: `[1]` encrypt→decrypt now measures 48 bits, and the encoder's own round trip `[0]`
is 1.3e-14. The O(N²) double-precision canonical embedding is now the **next floor**, so measuring
much beyond ~46 bits would need a higher-precision reference encoder, not a better scheme.

## Performance

Same four metrics as exp_Tb001, same definitions, same tooling (`tools/traces/busy_metrics.py`), so
the numbers belong in one table with that experiment's. `G=8`, `B=1`, `S=1`. Every row is a single
execution with a **verified 1-1 dispatch map** — host dispatch count equal to trace dispatch count,
every dispatch present with all 5 RISCs, contiguous.

| | baseline | +2-prime +centred | **+eval I/O (default)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| ciphertext I/O | coefficient | coefficient | **evaluation** |
| scale | 1 prime, non-centred | 2 primes, centred | 2 primes, centred |
| dispatches | 610 | 666 | **582** |
| transforms | 96 | 96 | **78** |
| wall time (profiled) | 15.763 ms | 16.325 ms | **14.297 ms** |
| busy span | 9.234 ms | 9.586 ms | **8.385 ms** |
| host device busy | 9.310 ms | 9.668 ms | **8.475 ms** |
| **critical path busy** | 8.419 ms | 8.688 ms | **7.600 ms** |
| precision (max / rms) | 11.1 / 16.4 bits | 42.4 / 45.2 bits | 42.4 / 45.2 bits |

Un-profiled host wall for the same three: 9.166 / 9.520 / 8.336 ms; un-profiled host device busy
9.198 / 9.560 / 8.378 ms. Turning the profiler on costs ~70% of wall time and ~1.6% of host device
busy, which is why the trace-derived pair is anchored to the host pair measured on the *same* replay.

**The net is +31 bits of precision and 9.7% less critical path busy than the starting point.**

Per HE operation, which is where the movement is legible:

| HE op | baseline | +2-prime +centred | +eval I/O |
|---|---|---|---|
| input (coeff→eval) | 1.936 ms | 1.935 ms | — |
| tensor | 0.458 | 0.458 | 0.459 |
| keyswitch | 4.736 | 4.724 | 4.728 |
| output (eval→coeff) | 1.001 | 1.003 | — |
| rescale | 0.245 | 0.527 | 2.373 |
| combine | 0.042 | 0.042 | 0.041 |
| **total** | **8.419** | **8.688** | **7.600** |

Two things to read off it. First, precision cost **+0.269 ms and nothing else** — `keyswitch`,
`tensor` and `combine` do not move, and the entire delta is `rescale` +0.282 ms, so the accounting
closes. Second, eval I/O does not simply delete the 1.936 ms input phase: it also deletes the 1.003 ms
output phase and *moves* both costs into `rescale`, which rises to 2.373 ms. The net saving is
1.092 ms, which is 18 transforms at the measured ~61 µs each.

### The transform accounting

| contract | in | ks→coeff | mod-up | mod-down | rescale + out | total | dispatches |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| coefficient, 1-prime | 32 | 8 | 16 | 24 | 16 | 96 | 610 |
| coefficient, 2-prime | 32 | 8 | 16 | 24 | 16 | 96 | 666 |
| **evaluation, 2-prime** | **0** | 8 | 16 | 24 | 30 | **78** | **582** |
| evaluation, 1-prime | 0 | 8 | 16 | 24 | 16 | 64 | 468 |

The input transform still runs **on device** — the same four-step NTT, the same kernels — it is just
encryption-side work rather than multiply work, so it happens once at setup, outside the trace capture
and outside every number above. That is exp_Tb001's contract exactly.

Note the interaction in the last two rows: in **coefficient** form a drop step costs *zero*
transforms however many primes it drops, so widening `Δ` is free there. In **evaluation** form each
step costs `1 INTT + d NTT`, and there are now two steps — 30 transforms against 16. So the two-prime
scale eats about half of what eval I/O would otherwise save (−18 instead of −32), and the two choices
are not independent. Both are worth having anyway: the precision is worth far more than 0.3 ms.

## What runs on device

Every numbered block in `device_stage()` is lifted **verbatim** from `host/experiments/exp_tb001.cpp` /
`exp_tb001b.cpp` — the optimised, validated versions (fused 8-partial contraction, SFPI twiddle,
width-correct FBC, single-source `bconv_one`). Retyping them is how they drift.

Each stage is fed **reference inputs** so a fault localises to that stage; only the last runs the
whole chain from its own intermediates.

```
[D1] device NTT (coeff->eval)  vs host                        0/65536  [PASS]
[D2] device INTT(eval->coeff)  vs host                        0/65536  [PASS]
[D3] device tensor (pointwise mod Q) vs reference            0/196608  [PASS]
[D4] device relinearise (dnum=2 hybrid key-switch)            0/65536  [PASS]
[D5] device rescale (drop 2 prime(s), centred=1)              0/49152  [PASS]
     chain cost: 582 program dispatches, 78 transforms (evaluation I/O, G=8)
[D6] WHOLE MULTIPLY on device vs the FIXED case              0/98304  [PASS]
[D7] decode(decrypt(device out)) vs z1(.)z2   max=1.675e-13  rms=2.724e-14  ~42.4 bits  [PASS]
```

`[D7]`'s numbers match `[4b]`'s to every digit, which is the check that the device chain reproduced
the host result bit-for-bit and not merely to within the tolerance. All four configurations
(`{1,2}-prime × {coeff,eval} I/O`) are bit-exact against their own fixture.

A measurement run (`TG000_TRACE` / `TG000_DRAIN`) **skips** `[D1]`–`[D5]`: their dispatches would
contaminate the capture, and their buffers would be allocated before the pool and shift every cached
address. exp_Tb001 does the same for the same reason.

## The fixed reference case

`reference/he/tg000_case.bin` carries the primes, the secret key, both evaluation keys, the
coefficient-form input ciphertexts, the expected output, and the slot vectors `z₁`, `z₂`. The device
is checked against **bytes on disk**, so a failure reproduces on another card and a host-side bug
cannot cancel a device-side one. Regenerate with `TG000_GEN=<path>`.

3.3 MB, of which the two evaluation keys are 1.6 MB (`dnum × QP × 2` polynomials) and the three
ciphertexts 1.5 MB. Storing the expected output rather than recomputing it is the point of the file,
so it is not compressible without giving that up.

## Running it

Host only — no device, ~10 s:

```sh
./build/exp_tg000 0
```

| flag | what it does |
|---|---|
| `TG000_DEV=1` | the device ladder `[D1]`–`[D5]` |
| `TG000_CHAIN=1` | `[D6]`/`[D7]` — the whole multiply from its own intermediates (needs `TG000_DEV`) |
| `TG000_TRACE=1` | capture + replay, and the two host-measured latency metrics (needs `TG000_DEV`) |
| `TG000_PROF=1` | profile ONE traced replay and print the `busy_metrics.py` command (needs `TG000_TRACE`) |
| `TG000_DRAIN=1` | a full trace of one multiply with no dropped markers, for the per-kernel breakdown |
| `TG000_LABELS=<path>` | per-dispatch HE-op labels, to join on `seq` with `dispatch_csv.py` |
| `TG000_NDROP=1` | one-prime scale (the low-precision A/B) |
| `TG000_NOCENTRE=1` | non-centred rescale remainder (the other half of the A/B) |
| `TG000_COEFF_IO=1` | coefficient-form I/O, putting the 32 input NTTs back inside the multiply |
| `TG000_NOISE=1` | slot-error distribution, coefficient-domain decomposition, both predictions |
| `TG000_ZEROERR=1` | drop the LWE error — isolates the scheme's own approximation error |
| `TG000_ITERS=<n>` | timing iterations (default 5) |
| `TG000_GEN=<path>` | regenerate the fixed case |

Correctness, end to end on device:

```sh
source ./env.sh

```

The four latency metrics:

```sh
NTT_CORES=8 TT_METAL_DEVICE_PROFILER=1 TG000_DEV=1 TG000_TRACE=1 TG000_PROF=1 \
    TG000_LABELS=/tmp/tg000_labels.csv ./build/exp_tg000 0
# then run the busy_metrics.py line it prints, with --expect / --wall-ms / --host-busy-ms filled in
python3 tools/traces/dispatch_csv.py <csv> --expect 582 --labels /tmp/tg000_labels.csv --out /tmp/tg000.csv
```

The A/B that produced the precision table (host only, ~20 s):

```sh

TG000_NOISE=1 ./build/exp_tg000 0
```

A fixture carries its `ndrop`/`centre` in the header and is **rejected** by a run configured
differently — a fixture built for another rescale is a different ciphertext, not a stale one.

## Relationship to the other programs

All three share `kernels/experiments/exp_tb001/` deliberately, so a kernel change is measured against all of them
at once and they cannot drift.

| | role |
|---|---|
| `exp_tb001` | **performance vehicle** — cost model, bit-exact against a CPU oracle, 552 dispatches / 7.494 ms |
| `exp_tb001b` | **BFV correctness vehicle** — decrypt-verified, and the reason `baseconv_sk.cpp` exists |
| `exp_tg000` | **CKKS correctness vehicle** — decrypt+decode-verified, no kernel of its own |

exp_Tg000 needing no new kernel is the substantive result about the kernel tree: everything CKKS
requires was already there, because exp_Tb001 had been modelling CKKS's op graph all along. What was
missing was never a kernel — it was a scheme.

## Not done

* **Depth is 1.** One multiply, `L=8 → 6`. A chain would need level tracking through `add_ct`, a
  policy for operands at different levels, and enough `Q` for the levels — at a 56-bit scale, 8 limbs
  buys two multiplications, not many.
* **`keyswitch` is 62% of the critical path** (4.728 ms of 7.600) and has had no attention. exp_Tb001's
  outstanding leads apply directly: merging runs of `modmul_sfpi_full`, a fused evk multiply-accumulate,
  folding the forward twiddle into `fusedC`'s read.
* **The encoder is the precision floor now**, not the scheme — see the end of *Precision*. Going past
  ~46 bits needs a higher-precision reference embedding, which is a host-side numerics job.
* **No `B>1` batching.** Every measurement here is `G=8, B=1, S=1`.
* **The per-kernel `TG000_DRAIN` capture is wired but not tabulated** in this doc; the numbers quoted
  are from the traced replay, which is the same execution the host metrics come from.
